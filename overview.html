FIT2109: How to Learn This Unit

Applied Computing Skills · Monash University

This unit covers tools and practices that every software project relies on: the shell, text processing, version control, debugging, performance, and reproducible environments. You will use these skills in every unit and job that follows — but only if you build the habit of using them yourself.

Four things you should understand from day one

1

We teach concepts — you practise everywhere

Workshops introduce the core ideas. The real learning happens when you apply them: in your applied sessions, coursework assessments, and in other units where you reach for a tool you saw here. Passive reading is not enough — you need to type the commands, break things, and fix them.

2

Read before you come to class

Each week has pre-reading materials linked from the unit page. Workshop time is not a lecture — it is where we work through the hard parts together, ask questions, and go deeper. If you arrive without having read, you will spend the session catching up on material you could have absorbed at your own pace beforehand.

3

There is far more to learn than we can cover — and that is the point

The shell ecosystem alone could fill a year-long course. We have chosen the concepts that unlock the rest: once you understand why pipes exist, or what a process is, the thousands of tools built on top of them start to make sense. Our goal is to give you a foundation you can extend on your own.

4

Do not try to memorise commands

No one does. Experienced engineers spend their careers looking things up. What we actually assess is your ability to break a problem down, identify what kind of tool would help, find its usage, and adapt it. These meta-skills — reading documentation, analysing errors, thinking systematically — transfer to every technology you will ever touch.

5

Be curious — explore and share what you find

The best way to learn a tool is to poke at it: try a flag you have not seen, pipe two commands together and see what happens, break something on purpose and figure out why. Curiosity is what turns a beginner into someone who actually knows the tool. That said — some commands are irreversible. Never run rm -rf * or any variant unless you are absolutely certain what it will delete, because there is no undo.

6

Share what you find

When you discover something interesting, post it on Ed so your classmates can learn from it. If you spot an error or improvement in the course materials, we genuinely welcome pull requests — the repository is open at github.com/yqtian-se/fit2109-public.

A practical habit that separates good engineers When a command fails, read the error message carefully before searching for an answer. Most errors contain the fix. Building the reflex to read before reaching for Google is one of the most valuable things you can take from this unit.

Topics covered this semester

These are the weeks taught in this unit. Each links to its pre-reading overview.

Week 1
Introduction to the Shell
Navigation, files, permissions, pipes, and $PATH.
Week 2
Shell Evaluation, Scripting & Remote Access
Quoting, expansion, scripts, SSH, and file transfer.
Week 6
Reproducible Environments
Virtual machines, Docker containers, package managers, and lockfiles.
Week 8
Debugging — Systematic Bug Hunting
Mental models for finding bugs, debuggers, and logging.
Week 9
Profiling & Performance
Measuring where time is spent and knowing when optimisation is worth it.
Week 12
Capstone / Review
Bringing it together across tools, workflows, and your own projects.
